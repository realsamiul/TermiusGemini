## Claim versus reality

Mount Holyoke’s official claims are serious, but they need to be read carefully. The strongest claim is that the college “promises to meet the demonstrated financial need” of each admitted student and that this includes international students. That is **better language than many peers use**, and it is reinforced by the one-time application / four-year aid framework. The caveat is that the college’s own aid pages also say that international packages **typically include loans and campus employment**, so “meeting need” is **not** equivalent to “full grant” or “no-loan”. citeturn47view0turn25view0turn24view0

The second claim is that **45% of incoming international students** receive aid, with an **average need-based award of $58,686** and an **average merit award of $17,500**. The reality check is that this still means **more than half of incoming internationals receive neither a need-based package nor merit scholarship**. Moreover, the merit figure is far below the amount a high-need Bangladeshi applicant usually needs. So the existence of merit aid is positive, but merit by itself is rarely transformative for a student who needs $55k–$75k per year. citeturn25view1

The third claim is affordability through the family-contribution framework. The reality is again mixed. Mount Holyoke says aid generally covers the difference between costs and the college-calculated family contribution, but it also explicitly warns that families usually retain responsibility for both billed and non-billed costs, including books, personal expenses, and travel. That warning matters for Bangladesh-based families because international travel and visa-related costs are real, not hypothetical. citeturn24view0

The most important missing piece is the current **Common Data Set Section H6** detail for nonresident-alien grants. Mount Holyoke does host current CDS section files on its official CDS page, but in this environment the current H-section PDF was served through Google Drive in a way that made the exact H6 numeric cells non-verifiable. That missing H6 audit means two things at once. First, it lowers confidence in any exact “coverage rate” statement for all internationals across all undergraduate years. Second, it does **not** erase the more current official signals already on the site: the college says it meets admitted students’ documented need, the current class profile says it meets the full documented need of admitted students who apply, and the at-a-glance page gives current incoming international award proxies. For a high-need Bangladeshi student, the crucial question is not “any support” but **meaningful support**. ## Honest verdict

**What this school actually is for a high-need Bangladeshi student.** Mount Holyoke is a **real** option, not a fantasy option. If you are admitted **with need-based aid**, the school looks considerably more workable than many U.S. colleges that talk warmly about internationals but quietly gap them; but you should still assume that the admissions process itself is financially sensitive for internationals. citeturn30view1turn47view0turn25view0

**The gap between what the school claims and what the data shows.** The school’s language about meeting need is strong and unusually reassuring, but the fine print matters: the “need met” package for internationals typically includes a **loan** and **campus employment**, and the current exact CDS H6 recipient/budget numbers could not be independently verified from the official file in this environment. So this is **not** a no-loan promise school for internationals, and it is **not** a school where you should assume every admitted international is being fully grant-funded. citeturn25view0turn16search1

**The realistic scenario for a student at SAT 1480 with $60,000/year need.** If you are a strong 1480 applicant with credible leadership, strong writing, and either A Levels/IB or very well-documented national-curriculum exams, Mount Holyoke is worth a serious application. If admitted with need-based aid, the package is plausibly close enough to workable that a small residual gap might be bridged. If admitted without aid, or with only a modest merit award, it is not workable. That is the real fork in the road. citeturn48view0turn25view1turn25view0turn47view0

**Who should apply.** A Bangladeshi applicant who is academically strong, genuinely fits a women’s / gender-diverse liberal-arts environment, can present a convincing leadership or community-impact narrative, and is comfortable with a process that uses the CSS Profile and translated family documentation should absolutely keep Mount Holyoke in contention. The case is especially good if you value a visible Bangladeshi community on campus, because Bangladesh is already among the most represented non-U.S. countries in the newest class and BONDHU is active. citeturn48view0turn36view2

**Who should not rely on it.** If your family needs a guaranteed **no-loan** full ride, or if your case depends on the college clearly understanding **SSC/HSC** without any ambiguity, Mount Holyoke is not crisp enough to be trusted as a mainline certainty. You can apply, but you should pair it with schools that are either explicitly international-need-blind, more transparent about international-funded counts, or publicly clearer on national-curriculum evaluation. citeturn25view0turn47view0

My bottom-line verdict is therefore: **apply if you are truly competitive, but do not build your whole strategy around Mount Holyoke alone**. It is one of the more promising small-college options for a high-need Bangladeshi student, yet still a place where funding scarcity and policy ambiguity matter. citeturn30view1turn25view1turn48view0
