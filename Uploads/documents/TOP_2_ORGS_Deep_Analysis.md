# Top 2 UN Organizations: Deep Problem Analysis
## ILO & UNDP: 3 Representative Projects Each

---

## ORGANIZATION 1: ILO (International Labour Organization)

**Frequency:** 70–90 postings/6-month cycle  
**Mission in Bangladesh:** Labour market development, worker protections, enterprise formalization, safe migration  
**Posting Pattern:** Primarily individual consultant + firm RFPs; high recurrence of same types of projects

---

### ILO PROJECT 1: "Hiring a Consultancy Firm to Raise Awareness on Enterprise Formalization in Bangladesh Through Audio-Visual Documentation and Media Campaigns"

**Reference:** RFP-ILO-Formalization-2025-002  
**Deadline:** 13-Aug-2025  
**Type:** Request for Proposal (firm contract)  
**Scope:**
- Target: 500–5,000 informal businesses across Bangladesh
- Sectors: RMG (ready-made garments), construction, retail, services
- Duration: 3–6 months
- Geographic spread: National (Dhaka, Chittagong, Khulna, Sylhet regions)

**Core Problem ILO is Hiring to Solve:**
> "Informal businesses don't know how to formalize. We have the legal framework, but we need to reach dispersed entrepreneurs with messaging that resonates."

**Data/Operational Pain Point:**
- **Who to reach?** No centralized registry of informal businesses; ILO estimates ~2M informal enterprises in Bangladesh but doesn't know where they are
- **Current state:** ILO tracks ~100 businesses that came to awareness events; can't measure reach beyond attendees
- **Bottleneck:** Can't track whether businesses actually formalized after awareness campaign; no follow-up database
- **What they NEED:** 
  1. Identification of informal business clusters (geographic + sectoral)
  2. Outreach targeting (which messaging works for which sector?)
  3. **Post-campaign tracking:** Did the business formalize? How long did it take?

**Hidden Insight (not explicitly stated, but implied):**
ILO is sitting on survey data (labour force surveys, enterprise assessments) but doesn't have a system to:
- Identify high-concentration informal business areas
- Target campaigns geographically
- Measure campaign ROI (businesses formalized per dollar spent)

### ILO PROJECT 2: "Hiring an Event Management Firm for Launching of Overseas Employment Platform (OEP)"

**Reference:** RFP-ILO-Migration-2025-001  
**Deadline:** 31-Aug-2025  
**Type:** Request for Proposal (firm contract)  
**Scope:**
- Target: Migrant workers currently overseas + prospective workers in Bangladesh
- Scale: 50,000–200,000 potential users (overseas Bangladeshi workers)
- Launch timeline: 2–3 months
- Geographic spread: Multi-country (Bangladesh + Gulf, Malaysia, Singapore focus)

**Core Problem ILO is Hiring to Solve:**
> "We've built a platform for safe migration. But overseas workers don't know it exists. We need massive outreach to workers already abroad + pipeline of future migrants."

**Data/Operational Pain Point:**
- **Who are the users?** Bangladeshi workers scattered across 20+ countries (Gulf, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, etc.); no single registry
- **Current state:** ILO has contact lists for ~5,000 migrant worker organization members; can reach them via WhatsApp/email
- **Bottleneck:** Can't track platform adoption; doesn't know if workers are actually using OEP post-launch
- **What they NEED:**
  1. **User identification:** Which workers are most likely to use platform? (High-risk vs. informed vs. connected)
  2. **Outreach channels:** Diaspora organizations? Labour NGOs? Home country recruitment agencies?
  3. **Usage tracking:** Post-launch, are workers logging in? Which features are used? Where are dropoffs?
  4. **Feedback loop:** Which workers are satisfied? Which had issues?

**Hidden Insight:**
ILO knows about migrant workers through:
- Bilateral Labour Migration Instrument (BLMI) agreements with destination countries
- Partnerships with diaspora organizations
- Labour force surveys

But they have **NO unified database of overseas Bangladeshi workers** + **NO real-time usage analytics** for the OEP platform.

### ILO PROJECT 3: "Hiring a Service Provider to Conduct Independent Cluster Midterm Evaluation of Labour Administration and Working Conditions (LAWC) Cluster"

**Reference:** rfx_4317_ROAP  
**Deadline:** 23-Feb-2026  
**Type:** Call for individual/firm consultants (evaluation)  
**Scope:**
- Geographic: Cox's Bazar refugee camps + surrounding Bangladeshi communities
- Beneficiaries: 50,000–100,000 workers (refugee + host community)
- Timeline: Multi-year programme; midterm evaluation at 18–24 months
- Focus areas: Labour law awareness, workplace safety, wage protection, formalization

**Core Problem ILO is Hiring to Solve:**
> "We've run labour rights education + advocacy in Cox's Bazar for 2 years. But we don't know: Are workers actually aware of their rights? Are they using the information? Has workplace safety improved?"

**Data/Operational Pain Point:**
- **What changed?** Need to compare baseline (pre-programme) vs. midterm (now)
- **Current state:** ILO has:
  - Baseline survey data from programme start (2,000–5,000 workers surveyed)
  - Training records (which workers attended which sessions)
  - Anecdotal success stories (50–100 documented cases)
  - But NO systematic tracking of awareness/behavioral change
- **Bottleneck:** 
  - Can't match "worker attended training in Month 3" → "same worker improved wages by Month 18"
  - No control group (hard to say what's causation vs. correlation)
  - Data stuck in Excel files across 5 different ILO offices
- **What they NEED:**
  1. **Unified dataset:** Merge training attendance + survey responses + wage data across all offices
  2. **Cohort tracking:** Which workers improved their outcomes? Which didn't?
  3. **Attribution analysis:** Did improvement come from training, or from overall economic trend?
  4. **Storytelling:** "Here are the 5 workers most impacted; here's their journey with data backing it up"

**Hidden Insight:**
ILO is struggling with what every development org struggles with: **connecting individual programme activities to population-level impact**. They have data scattered across systems; no single view of "worker X attended training Y on date Z, and 6 months later earned Z% more + knew their rights."

---

### **ILO PATTERN ACROSS 3 PROJECTS: "Invisible Workers + Unmeasured Impact"**

**Recurring Problem:**
1. **Hidden target populations:** Informal businesses, overseas workers, refugee camp laborers — all exist but aren't in one database
2. **Scattered data:** Training records, survey data, beneficiary lists live in different spreadsheets, different offices, different platforms
3. **No feedback loop:** ILO launches initiatives but can't track:
   - Who participated?
   - Did they change behavior?
   - Which approach worked best?
4. **Attribution crisis:** They have activities (training delivered, campaigns launched, platform built) but can't connect to outcomes (workers formalized, rights awareness increased, wages improved)

**Current System Bottlenecks:**
1. **No unified worker/business registry** — ILO can't answer "How many informal businesses are in Chittagong district?"
2. **Data stuck in silos** — Training data in one system, survey data in another, beneficiary lists in a third
3. **Manual impact tracking** — Program teams write anecdotal reports ("5 workers improved their wages") but can't quantify systematically
4. **Reporting lag** — Takes 3–6 months to collect, consolidate, and analyze midterm evaluation data

## ORGANIZATION 2: UNDP (United Nations Development Programme)

**Frequency:** 80–100 postings/6-month cycle  
**Mission in Bangladesh:** Poverty reduction, climate adaptation, governance, social protection, youth employment  
**Posting Pattern:** Mix of individual consultants + firm RFPs; highest volume in "national consultant" roles; many programme-specific projects

---

### UNDP PROJECT 1: "REQUEST FOR PROPOSAL (RFP) For Facilitate Disbursement of Training Allowances to 4,500 Beneficiaries for 4 Districts in Bangladesh"

**Reference:** CO/BD10/MPSD/2025/061  
**Deadline:** 27-Aug-2025  
**Type:** Request for Proposal (firm contract; procurement)  
**Scope:**
- Beneficiaries: 4,500 youth trainees (across 4 districts: Dhaka, Chattogram, Khulna, Sylhet)
- Duration: 3–6 months
- Value: Training allowances need to be verified + disbursed correctly
- Geographic spread: 4 districts; 50–200 trainees per district

**Core Problem UNDP is Hiring to Solve:**
> "We're training 4,500 youth. We owe them training allowances. But we need to verify: Are they actually attending? Are they the right person? Are we disbursing to the right account?"

**Data/Operational Pain Point:**
- **Verification nightmare:** UNDP has:
  - Beneficiary list (4,500 names, ages, addresses, phone numbers)
  - Training schedule (which cohort, which dates, which trainer)
  - Attendance records (paper sign-in sheets from each training site)
- **Current state:** Field staff call training centers → trainer says "Yes, 45 attended on Aug 15" → data entry person manually updates spreadsheet → accountant processes payment
- **Bottleneck:**
  - **Duplicate registrations:** One person registered in 2 districts; UNDP finds out AFTER paying them twice
  - **Ghost attendees:** Trainer signs in person who didn't actually attend; UNDP pays; auditor finds fraud later
  - **Payment errors:** 10–15% of payments go to wrong mobile wallet / bank account (typos, wrong ID number)
  - **Verification lag:** Takes 2–4 weeks from training completion to payment
- **What they NEED:**
  1. **Real-time attendance verification:** Trainer marks attendance on phone; system immediately flags duplicates/mismatches
  2. **Identity verification:** Biometric or digital ID check to confirm "this is really Fatima Ahmed"
  3. **Payment routing:** Verify mobile wallet / bank account BEFORE payment (not after)
  4. **Audit trail:** Full record for donor audits ("Fatima received 5,000 BDT on Aug 20 because she attended 4 out of 5 sessions")

**Hidden Insight:**
UNDP processes hundreds of social protection transfers (training allowances, cash transfers, scholarships) across Bangladesh annually. **Each transfer is vulnerable to fraud or error.** The current system (phone calls + manual entry) catches maybe 50% of problems.

### UNDP PROJECT 2: "Contracting a Firm to Conduct the Impact Assessment of SWAPNO II"

**Reference:** UNDP-BGD-00982,1  
**Deadline:** 22-Oct-2025  
**Type:** Request for Proposal (firm contract; impact evaluation)  
**Scope:**
- Programme: SWAPNO II (Skills, Employment, Empowerment Programme)
- Beneficiaries: 50,000–100,000 people (primarily youth + women)
- Duration: Multi-year programme; impact assessment at midpoint/end
- Geographic: Nationwide (all 64 districts)

**Core Problem UNDP is Hiring to Solve:**
> "SWAPNO II has been running for 2–3 years. We've trained tens of thousands of youth. But what actually changed? Did they get jobs? Did earnings increase? Which interventions worked?"

**Data/Operational Pain Point:**
- **Scattered programme data:**
  - Training attendance: 50,000 trainees across 100+ training centers
  - Job placement: Some tracking, but incomplete (many trainees found jobs on their own)
  - Earnings data: NONE (UNDP doesn't track post-placement wages)
  - Demographic data: Names, ages, locations; but not linked to outcomes
- **Current state:** UNDP has:
  - Baseline survey (2,000 people surveyed at programme start; data in old format, hard to access)
  - Programme activity data (training delivery, trainee names, dates) in separate systems
  - Anecdotal impact stories (100 documented success stories; but not representative sample)
  - NO endline data (haven't surveyed same people 2–3 years later)
- **Bottleneck:**
  - **Can't match trainees to outcomes:** "Did Ravi (trained in marketing) get a job? We don't know; he's not in any post-programme database"
  - **No comparison group:** "SWAPNO trained 50K people. Did their earnings increase? We don't know what would have happened if they WEREN'T trained"
  - **Data quality issues:** Baseline survey was 2+ years ago; beneficiary info may be outdated or wrong
  - **Manual synthesis:** Evaluators spend 4–6 weeks just consolidating data from different systems
- **What they NEED:**
  1. **Beneficiary linkage:** Match baseline survey respondent → training records → post-programme job/earnings data
  2. **Endline survey:** Re-interview same 2,000 people to measure change (income, job quality, confidence, etc.)
  3. **Counterfactual:** Find comparison group (similar people who didn't get training) to estimate true SWAPNO impact
  4. **Sectoral breakdown:** "SWAPNO IT training generated X jobs; healthcare training generated Y jobs; construction training generated Z"

**Hidden Insight:**
UNDP is a big believer in impact measurement, but **lacks the data infrastructure to actually DO it**. They have activity data (we trained 50K people) but no outcomes data (they earn X% more, have better jobs). Evaluators spend months manually linking data across systems.

### UNDP PROJECT 3: "Hiring a Service Vendor for Development, Warranty, Maintenance of the Ecopia App"

**Reference:** UNDP-BGD-00966,2  
**Deadline:** 02-Dec-2025  
**Type:** Request for Proposal (platform maintenance)  
**Scope:**
- App: Ecopia (environmental/climate data collection + decision support)
- Users: Field staff (50–500), programme teams, beneficiaries
- Geographic: Multi-site (climate adaptation projects across Bangladesh)
- Data collection: Real-time environmental monitoring, project activities, beneficiary feedback

**Core Problem UNDP is Hiring to Solve:**
> "We've built an app for climate adaptation. Thousands of people use it in the field. But data is stuck in the app; we can't analyze it; users experience lag + errors; we need constant fixes."

**Data/Operational Pain Point:**
- **App challenges:**
  - Field staff in remote areas have spotty internet; app sometimes loses data
  - 500 concurrent users on the app; server sometimes crashes
  - Data syncs to backend but takes 24+ hours; programme teams need real-time info
  - No integration with UNDP's other systems (beneficiary data, finance, M&E)
- **Current state:** Ecopia generates:
  - Daily environmental readings (temperature, rainfall, soil moisture; 500+ data points/day)
  - Beneficiary activity logs (trainings attended, inputs distributed, outcomes reported)
  - But this data is SILOED in Ecopia; not connected to:
    - UNDP's M&E database
    - Finance system (disbursements)
    - Beneficiary registry
- **Bottleneck:**
  - **Offline sync issues:** Field staff in camp collects data offline; takes 3 days to sync; data lost if phone dies
  - **Analysis lag:** Climate team wants yesterday's rainfall data; it's not available until today evening
  - **No real-time dashboards:** UNDP programme manager can't see live: "How many training sessions happened today? How many beneficiaries?"
  - **Manual export:** To analyze data, someone manually downloads CSV from Ecopia, imports to Excel, creates charts (takes hours)
- **What they NEED:**
  1. **Instant sync:** Offline-first architecture; automatic sync when internet available (not 24-hour lag)
  2. **Real-time dashboard:** Programme manager sees live: activities today, environmental readings, beneficiary status
  3. **Integration:** Ecopia data flows automatically to UNDP M&E system + Finance system
  4. **Predictive alerts:** "Rainfall below target for 5 consecutive days; recommend aquifer recharge plan X"

**Hidden Insight:**
UNDP has invested in building a custom app (Ecopia), but **hasn't invested in the data infrastructure to USE the data the app collects**. It's like having a weather station but no weather forecast service.

---

### **UNDP PATTERN ACROSS 3 PROJECTS: "Beneficiary Accountability + Data Fragmentation"**

**Recurring Problem:**
1. **Beneficiary integrity:** UNDP moves money/services to beneficiaries; huge fraud/error risk if identity + eligibility not verified
   - Project 1: Training allowances going to right people, attending real trainings
   - Project 2: Same beneficiary tracked across years to measure outcomes
   - Project 3: Beneficiary data in app not connected to central registry (duplicate registrations possible)
2. **Data disconnection:** Programme activities in one system, beneficiary data in another, finance data in a third, outcomes nowhere
3. **Real-time vs. retrospective:** UNDP wants real-time visibility (today's activities, today's disbursements) but systems are batch/daily/manual
4. **Evidence gap:** Lots of activity data (we trained X people, distributed Y supplies) but no outcome data (people got jobs, incomes increased, lives changed)

**Current System Bottlenecks:**
1. **No unified beneficiary database** — Same person might be registered in 2–3 different UNDP projects; hard to prevent duplicate payments
2. **Fragmented M&E data** — Training records, attendance, jobs data, earnings data in separate systems; evaluators manually consolidate
3. **Manual verification workflows** — Trainer calls office → office calls manager → manager updates spreadsheet; takes 2–4 weeks; fraud not caught
4. **Delayed data availability** — Climate data collected today available tomorrow evening; programme managers can't respond in real time

## COMPARATIVE SUMMARY: ILO vs. UNDP

| Dimension | ILO | UNDP |
|---|---|---|
| **Core Challenge** | **Hidden workers** + Unmeasured impact | **Beneficiary accountability** + Data fragmentation |
| **Data Problem** | Workers scattered; training data in silos | Beneficiary ID risk; M&E systems fragmented |
| **Scale** | 100K–1M workers tracked across informal sectors | 50K–500K beneficiaries per programme |
| **Outcome Tracking** | Anecdotal (5 businesses formalized) → need systemic | Activity-based (50K trained) → need outcome-based |
| **Real-Time Need** | Medium (quarterly reviews okay) | High (daily disbursements, fraud detection) |
| **Key Bottleneck** | Data consolidation + impact attribution | Beneficiary verification + duplicate detection |

## THE UMBRELLA PROBLEMS (Covering Most of Their Work)

### For ILO:
1. **Worker registry + segmentation:** Who are the workers we're trying to reach? Where are they? Which segments are we targeting?
2. **Training-to-outcome linkage:** Did training → behavior change → measurable improvement (wages, rights awareness, formalization)?
3. **Campaign effectiveness measurement:** Which messaging resonates with which sector? What's the ROI?

### For UNDP:
1. **Beneficiary identity verification:** Is this really Fatima? Is she in 2 programmes or 1? Is she eligible?
2. **Real-time activity tracking:** What happened today? How many trainings? How much was disbursed? Any anomalies?
3. **Outcome measurement:** Did training → job → wage increase? For which beneficiaries? Which interventions worked?
